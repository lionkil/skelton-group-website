First-principles materials modelling and structural dynamics

We are a theory group based in the Department of Chemistry at the University of Manchester, UK.

We use a wide variety of theoretical modelling techniques to study materials ranging from inorganic semiconductors and f-element materials to molecular crystals and "hybrid" inorganic/organic systems.

Our research

We are particularly interested in the structural dynamics of solids and their effect on material properties. Our work in this area includes understanding the fundamentals of heat transport, devising new engineering strategies to control it, and predicting the structural, mechanical and electrical properties of materials at elevated temperature. We have a long-standing interest in improving materials for energy applications, including thermoelectric power generation and, more recently, photovoltaics and battery materials. We also study f-element materials, with an interest in how their electronic structure influences magnetism and other physical properties.

Our work applies and extends established theories to address new problems, with strong focus on making useful methods available to the community through open-source software and on strengthening links between theory and experiment. Infrared and Raman modelling is a particular focus: we predict vibrational spectra to help explain experimental measurements and connect them to atomic structure and dynamics. We regularly work with experimental groups to validate theoretical models and to help get the best insight from measurements.
